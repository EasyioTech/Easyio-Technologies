## 7 Fatal Mistakes Kashmiri Businesses Make When Hiring Software Companies

Before signing any software contract, make sure you are not making one of these seven common mistakes:

### 1. Falling for the "Cheap WordPress Template" Trap
Many low-cost agencies in the Valley charge ₹5,000 to ₹15,000 for a website by purchasing a bloated, pre-made WordPress theme or installing a nulled PHP script. These templates are packed with thousands of lines of unused JavaScript, take 6 to 10 seconds to load on mobile networks, and are riddled with security vulnerabilities that leave your database exposed to malicious attacks. 

*Rule of thumb:* If an agency cannot build with modern, high-speed stacks like Next.js, React, and TypeScript, they are assembling templates, not engineering software.

### 2. Failing to Secure 100% Code & Intellectual Property Ownership
Certain software providers construct platforms on proprietary frameworks that prevent you from ever moving your software or database to another server. If you decide to end your business relationship with them, they hold your customer records and code hostage.

*Rule of thumb:* Always insist on a contract clause stating that **100% of the Git repository, database schema, design assets, and intellectual property belong exclusively to your company** upon milestone payments.

### 3. Not Testing for Mobile-First Performance
In Jammu and Kashmir, more than 75% of commercial website visits and employee data inputs occur on mobile smartphones. Many agencies design flashy desktop mockups that look impressive on a 27-inch monitor in an agency conference room, but break down completely when loaded on an Android smartphone connected to a 4G cellular network.

*Rule of thumb:* Demand live links to previous projects and run them on your personal phone. Test how quickly pages load, how easy the buttons are to tap with one thumb, and whether forms submit instantly.

### 4. Overlooking Offline-First Architecture
If your software requires an active internet connection for every single click, your business will grind to a halt the moment broadband is interrupted by adverse winter weather or regional network maintenance. 

*Rule of thumb:* Inquire specifically about how the system handles offline states. A professional application leverages Progressive Web App (PWA) service workers and client-side database caching (IndexedDB/SQLite) to queue transactions locally and synchronize silently with the cloud once connectivity resumes.

### 5. Ignoring Staff Usability and User Experience (UX)
The most sophisticated enterprise software is completely worthless if your employees refuse to use it. Many traditional enterprise platforms feature dense, cluttered interfaces reminiscent of 1990s accounting software. When software is frustrating to navigate, employees revert to writing notes on scraps of paper or maintaining unauthorized parallel Excel files.

*Rule of thumb:* Request a demonstration of the administrative interface. The dashboard should be clean, visual, and intuitive enough that a newly hired clerk can master it within two hours.

### 6. Overpaying for Unused SaaS Subscriptions
Off-the-shelf software vendors (like Zoho, Salesforce, or SAP) charge recurring monthly fees per active user. As your team expands from 5 to 50 employees, your annual software subscription costs skyrocket into lakhs of rupees every year—without ever owning the underlying software.

*Rule of thumb:* For specialized workflows (such as cold storage logistics, fruit mandi consignment tracking, or regional distribution), custom software pays for itself within 12 to 18 months by eliminating recurring licensing fees.

### 7. Hiring Based on Sales Talk Rather than Verifiable Engineering
Many agencies employ smooth-talking sales representatives who promise every conceivable feature without consulting their technical team. Once the contract is signed, the project is handed off to junior interns or outsourced to untraceable third-party contractors.

*Rule of thumb:* Insist on speaking directly with the lead software architect who will actually write and oversee your codebase before committing funds.

## The 6-Step Technical Due Diligence Framework

To evaluate prospective software firms objectively, follow this structured vetting process:

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                        THE 6-STEP SOFTWARE DUE DILIGENCE CHECKLIST                      |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| STEP 1: Inspect the Tech Stack (Modern Next.js/Postgres vs. Outdated PHP/WordPress)     |
| STEP 2: Verify Mobile Page Speed on Google PageSpeed Insights (Must score 90+)          |
| STEP 3: Validate Offline Sync Capabilities (PWA & Client-Side Storage)                  |
| STEP 4: Review Code & IP Ownership Terms in the Contract (100% Client Ownership)        |
| STEP 5: Check Domain Understanding (Can they explain your business model?)              |
| STEP 6: Confirm Ongoing Support & SLA Response Times in Writing                         |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Step 1: Inspect Their Technology Stack
Ask the development agency what tools and frameworks they intend to use. Here is a quick reference guide:
- **Red Flag Tech Stacks:** PHP 5.x, WordPress with 20+ plugins, Joomla, Drupal, jQuery-heavy templates, shared cPanel hosting.
- **Gold Standard Modern Stacks:** Next.js 15, React 19, TypeScript, Node.js, Python (FastAPI/Django), PostgreSQL, Redis, Docker, and managed cloud infrastructure (AWS, Google Cloud, DigitalOcean).

### Step 2: Validate Real-World Mobile Speed
Run previous client websites built by the firm through **Google PageSpeed Insights** (pagespeed.web.dev). 
- A score below 60 on mobile indicates bloated code and poor performance.
- A score of 90+ indicates an engineering team that understands Core Web Vitals, asset optimization, and edge caching.

### Step 3: Validate Domain Understanding
Before discussing pricing, ask the agency to explain your own business operations back to you. If you are an apple mandi trader in Sopore, ask them how their software will calculate arath commissions, biyana advances, and crate balances. If they do not know what an arath khata or a CA chamber pallet manifest is, they will spend months learning on your dime.

### Step 4: Demand Milestone-Based Payment Structures
Never pay 100% of a software project upfront. Professional software engineering firms operate on structured milestones linked to tangible deliverables:
- **Milestone 1 (25%):** Requirements analysis, system architecture, and interactive UI/UX wireframes.
- **Milestone 2 (25%):** Core database schema, authentication, and primary backend workflows.
- **Milestone 3 (25%):** Frontend integration, offline synchronization, and feature completion.
- **Milestone 4 (25%):** End-to-end testing, staff training, production deployment, and final IP handover.

## 10 Essential Questions to Ask Before Signing a Software Contract

Print this list or bring it to your next agency consultation:

1. *"Will our company own 100% of the source code and database upon final payment, or are we licensing it from you?"*
2. *"Can we review the live URL of a web application you built that scores above 90 on Google PageSpeed Insights?"*
3. *"How does this system behave if our broadband connection drops while staff are entering transactions?"*
4. *"Who will be our direct technical point of contact during development—a project manager or the lead software engineer?"*
5. *"Where will our database be hosted, and will we have root access credentials to the cloud server?"*
6. *"How are automated database backups managed, and what is your disaster recovery protocol?"*
7. *"Do you provide on-site, in-person training for our employees at our business premises?"*
8. *"What is your guaranteed response time under your Service Level Agreement (SLA) if an emergency bug halts billing?"*
9. *"What security protocols (e.g., role-based permissions, data encryption, SQL injection prevention) are implemented?"*
10. *"Can you provide contact details of two active business clients in Kashmir who currently use your software?"*
